## What ExpressVPN actually does (and doesn't)

ExpressVPN encrypts the connection between your device and one of its servers, and shows websites the server's IP address instead of yours. That's genuinely useful for:

- Securing your traffic on public Wi-Fi (coffee shops, airports, hotels).
- Hiding the destinations of your traffic from your ISP (they can still see that you're using a VPN, just not what you do through it).
- Getting a different regional IP address for some services.

It is **not**:

- A way to become "anonymous" online. See the dedicated section below — this matters more than most VPN marketing admits.
- A guarantee that every streaming service or blocked site will work, forever. Platforms actively detect and block known VPN IP ranges, and this changes over time.
- A complete security solution. It doesn't stop phishing, malware, weak passwords, or a compromised account.

## What no VPN — including this one — can do

- **It does not make you "anonymous" online.** It hides your IP address from the sites you visit and encrypts your traffic to its own server, but the provider can technically see your connection metadata, and any account you log into (email, banking, social media) still identifies you regardless of VPN use.
- **It doesn't guarantee bypassing every block or restriction.** Streaming platforms and some networks actively detect and block known VPN IP ranges; what works today may stop working after a platform update.
- **It doesn't protect you from phishing, malware, weak passwords, or a compromised account.** It's one layer of network-level protection, not a complete security solution.
- **No-logs claims depend on trusting the provider and the scope of their audits** — they meaningfully reduce risk versus not using a VPN at all, but they aren't an absolute guarantee.
