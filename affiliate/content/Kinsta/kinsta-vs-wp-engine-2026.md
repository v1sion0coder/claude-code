## H2: Feature-by-Feature Comparison

### H3: Infrastructure & Performance

Kinsta runs exclusively on Google Cloud Platform's premium tier network with per-site container isolation. WP Engine has historically run on a mix of its own data centers and major cloud providers, with its EverCache system handling page/object caching at the platform level. Independent benchmark results between the two shift over time and depend heavily on the specific site, theme, and plugin stack being tested — **treat any specific benchmark numbers (ours or anyone else's) as a snapshot, not a permanent ranking, and consider running your own test with a staging copy of your actual site if performance is a deciding factor.**

### H3: Dashboard & Developer Tools

Kinsta's MyKinsta dashboard is comparatively newer and is generally described as clean and intuitive, with the APM tool built directly in for diagnosing slow plugins or queries without a separate service. WP Engine's dashboard (and its newer agency-focused interface) is mature and well-documented, and WP Engine's free Local app is a genuinely popular local-development tool used widely across the WordPress community, including by developers who don't host with WP Engine at all.

### H3: Staging Environments

Both platforms include staging environments as a standard feature for testing changes before pushing live — this is table stakes at the premium managed-WordPress tier, and neither has a meaningful edge here beyond workflow/UI preference.

### H3: Migrations

Both offer free, assisted migrations for new customers, removing much of the friction of switching from another host. Actual migration complexity still depends on your site's size, plugin count, and any custom server configuration — budget some buffer time either way for anything beyond a simple site.

### H3: Pricing Structure

Both companies price primarily around monthly visits/traffic and number of sites, with resource allowances scaling by tier, and both charge overage fees if you exceed your plan's visit allowance. Neither should be treated as unlimited-traffic hosting at a fixed price — model your realistic (and peak) traffic against each plan's stated allowance. **Specific prices, visit limits, and overage rates for both providers change periodically — verify current numbers directly on [kinsta.com](https://kinsta.com) and wpengine.com before deciding; we're intentionally not quoting figures here that could be stale by the time you read this.**

### H3: Extras & Bundled Value

WP Engine has historically bundled free access to a library of premium StudioPress (Genesis) themes with hosting plans — a real cost saving if you'd otherwise buy premium themes separately. Kinsta doesn't bundle themes but includes its APM performance tool at no extra cost across plans. Confirm current bundled extras for both, as these offers change.

### H3: Agency Features

Both serve agencies well, but WP Engine's dedicated agency dashboard and long track record with larger agency/enterprise deployments give it a slight edge for agencies managing a large, complex portfolio of client sites. Kinsta's multi-site management is solid for small-to-mid-size agencies but is generally seen as less enterprise-oriented than WP Engine's dedicated agency tooling.

### H3: Support

Both are generally well-regarded for WordPress-literate live chat support, a real differentiator versus budget hosts. Individual experiences vary and change over time with company scale — recent user reviews on independent review sites (Trustpilot, G2) are worth checking close to your decision date, since support quality can shift as companies grow.
