## What Thinkific and Teachable Actually Are

**Thinkific** is a platform for building and selling online courses, coaching programs, and digital downloads. It's built around a drag-and-drop site/page builder, a course builder with a variety of lesson content types (video, text, quizzes, surveys, assignments), and built-in tools for memberships and communities on higher plans. It integrates with third-party email and marketing tools rather than trying to be an all-in-one marketing suite itself.

**Teachable** covers similar core ground — course hosting, quizzes, certificates, coaching/session scheduling, and payment processing — with a page builder that's generally considered a bit more template-driven and less flexible than Thinkific's, in exchange for being quicker to get a page live without fiddling with layout.

## Course & Page Builder Flexibility

This is the area creators disagree about most, because it comes down to personal workflow preference:

- **Thinkific's** builder gives you more granular control over page layout and design — useful if you have specific branding requirements or want your school to look distinct rather than templated.
- **Teachable's** builder leans more template-first — faster for creators who just want to pick a layout and fill it in, at the cost of some customization depth.

Neither is "wrong" here — creators who've used both often say the choice comes down to whether you enjoy tinkering with page design or would rather not think about it at all.

## Pricing & Fees — What to Actually Check Before Buying

Both platforms have historically structured pricing around monthly/annual tiers, with differences in:
- Whether transaction fees apply on top of your monthly plan cost (this has differed between the two, and between tiers, at various points).
- What's included at the entry paid tier vs. what requires an upper tier (e.g., course completion certificates, quizzes with grading, communities, or coaching/session tools).
- Whether a genuinely usable free plan exists to test the platform before paying.

We're intentionally not quoting specific dollar figures or fee percentages here, because both companies have changed pricing and fee structures more than once, and a stale number in a "2026" article is worse than no number at all. **Verify current pricing, transaction fees, and what's included per tier directly on thinkific.com/pricing and teachable.com/pricing before deciding.**

## Payments & Payouts

Both platforms support processing payments from buyers and paying creators out, typically via major payment processors. Payout timing, supported countries/currencies, and any processor-side fees vary and are worth checking directly against your own country and currency — this is exactly the kind of detail that looks fine in marketing copy but can matter a lot depending on where you and your students are located. **Verify supported countries and payout details on the official site for your situation.**

## Community & Membership Features

If part of your plan is running a cohort, membership site, or ongoing community (not just a one-off course), check current plan tiers on both platforms carefully — these features have historically been gated to higher tiers on both Thinkific and Teachable, and feature sets change. Don't assume a feature you saw in a screenshot or an old review is still included at the same tier today.

## Migration & Lock-In

Moving an existing course catalog, student list, and payment history from one platform to the other is real work — export/import isn't always 1:1, and you may need to manually rebuild some page designs. If you're currently on one platform and considering switching, factor in a realistic migration timeline before committing, rather than assuming it's a weekend project.

## Honest Limitations of Thinkific

To be fair to readers actually deciding, Thinkific isn't perfect:

- **Some genuinely useful features (communities, advanced memberships, more built-in marketing tools) sit behind higher-priced tiers**, so the free or entry plan alone may not cover everything you eventually want to do.
- **It isn't an all-in-one marketing platform** — plan on connecting a separate email marketing tool if that's central to your launch strategy; Thinkific integrates with these rather than replacing them.
- **The page builder's flexibility has a learning curve** — if you want a fully custom look, expect to spend real time in the builder rather than a five-minute setup.
- **Recurring affiliate commissions (if you're evaluating this as a promoter, not just a buyer) depend on referred customers staying paying Thinkific subscribers** — churn on the buyer's side reduces the actual long-term commission, which is a normal feature of any recurring-commission program, not a Thinkific-specific flaw, but worth knowing.

## Honest Limitations of Teachable

- **Less design flexibility** than Thinkific for creators who want a truly custom-branded school rather than a templated look.
- **Feature/fee structure has changed over the platform's history** — creators researching Teachable should check current terms directly rather than relying on older reviews (including, potentially, parts of this one by the time you read it).
- **Community/membership tooling has historically been less central to Teachable's product** than to some competitors — verify current capabilities if that's a requirement for you.

## Who Should Choose Thinkific

- You want more control over how your course pages and school website look, without hiring a developer.
- You want to start on a genuinely free plan and grow into paid tiers as your course business grows.
- You're building more than a single course — e.g., a broader school with multiple products, and want a builder that scales with that.

## Who Should Choose Teachable

- You want the fastest path from "I have course content" to "my course is live and sellable," and don't need deep page customization.
- You value Teachable's long track record specifically with solo creators and coaches.
- Design flexibility matters less to you than speed of setup.
