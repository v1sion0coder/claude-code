## The 3 costs that make up "a Webflow website"

Most people are surprised that Webflow pricing has **two separate layers**. Understand these and the confusion disappears:

### 1. The Workspace / Site plan (hosting your live site)

To publish a site on your own custom domain (`yoursite.com`), you need a paid **Site plan**. Webflow splits these roughly into:

- **Basic-type plans** — for a simple site with no blog/dynamic content (portfolio, landing page, brochure site).
- **CMS-type plans** — the moment you want a blog, case studies, a portfolio that pulls from a database, or any "collection" of repeating content, you move up to a CMS plan.
- **Business-type plans** — higher traffic limits and more CMS items, for busier sites.

👉 **This is the #1 hidden cost people miss:** if you plan to blog (which you should, for SEO), budget for a **CMS plan, not the cheapest Basic plan.**
*(Exact prices → verify on webflow.com.)*

### 2. Ecommerce (only if you're selling products)

If you want to sell physical or digital products directly through Webflow, that's a **separate Ecommerce plan** on top of the above. Honest note: for a serious store, many people find a dedicated platform like Shopify stronger — so only pay for Webflow Ecommerce if you specifically want Webflow's design control for a small catalog.
*(Exact prices → verify on webflow.com.)*

### 3. Your domain name

Webflow hosting is included in the Site plan, but the **domain itself** (`yoursite.com`) is usually bought separately (~a few dollars to ~$15–20/year at a registrar). You point it at Webflow. This is a small, once-a-year cost that's easy to forget.

## What you DON'T pay extra for (Webflow's advantage)

Unlike self-hosted WordPress, with Webflow you do **not** separately pay for:
- Hosting (it's bundled into the Site plan)
- SSL certificate (included)
- Security patching / server maintenance (handled for you)
- A pile of premium plugins to get basic features working

So the Site plan looks more expensive at first glance than "$3/month WordPress hosting," but the "$3 hosting" almost always grows once you add a premium theme, a page builder, a caching plugin, a security plugin, and backups. Webflow's all-in number is higher but more predictable.

## Honest downsides on cost

- **CMS tier is a jump.** The cheapest plan won't give you a blog — budget for CMS from the start if content/SEO is your goal.
- **Add-on creep is smaller than WordPress, but not zero** — extra CMS items, more traffic, or team seats can push you up a tier.
- **Migrating off later is work.** You're not locked in forever, but moving a live Webflow CMS site elsewhere is more involved than exporting a WordPress site — factor that into a long-term decision.

## FAQ

**Is Webflow free?**
There's a free tier for building and previewing on a `.webflow.io` URL. To publish on your own domain with full features you need a paid Site plan. (Verify current limits on webflow.com.)

**Why is Webflow more expensive than $3 WordPress hosting?**
Because the Site plan bundles hosting, SSL, security, and maintenance that WordPress makes you assemble (and pay for) separately. The gap narrows a lot once you add real WordPress plugins and managed hosting.

**Do I need the CMS plan?**
If you want a blog or any repeating content (portfolio items, case studies, products-as-content), yes. A plain Basic plan won't do dynamic content.

**Can I use my own domain?**
Yes, on a paid Site plan. You buy the domain separately (~$10–20/year at a registrar) and connect it. (Verify on webflow.com.)
