## H2: Feature-by-Feature Comparison

### H3: Control Panel

Hostinger uses its own custom-built hPanel, generally considered clean and modern but different from the cPanel most hosting tutorials and third-party tools assume. Bluehost generally uses cPanel (or a Bluehost-customized version of it), which means broader compatibility with the huge volume of existing cPanel tutorials and migration tools online. If you've used cPanel before, or expect to need cPanel-specific tools later, that favors Bluehost; if you don't care either way, hPanel is not a downside on its own.

### H3: Beginner Onboarding

Both target first-time site owners with guided setup flows and one-click WordPress installs. Hostinger's onboarding and AI site builder are generally seen as slightly more modern/streamlined; Bluehost's onboarding leans on its long history of WordPress-specific guidance and documentation. Neither requires coding knowledge to get a basic site live.

### H3: Pricing Structure

Both hosts follow the same industry pattern: **a low advertised price for an initial prepaid term (often 12–36 months), followed by a higher renewal price.** Neither company's marketing page emphasizes the renewal number as prominently as the intro number — that's a budget-hosting industry norm, not unique to either brand. **We are intentionally not quoting specific dollar figures for either host here, since intro pricing, renewal pricing, and included limits change frequently on both sides. Verify current intro price, current renewal price, and minimum term on hostinger.com and bluehost.com directly before buying**, and compare the renewal-term monthly cost, not just the ad price, between the two.

### H3: Resource Limits on Entry Plans

Both companies' cheapest shared plans impose limits on the number of websites, storage, and bandwidth/CPU resources — the specifics differ by provider and change over time. **Check the current specific limits on each provider's plan comparison page** rather than assuming "unlimited" language on either site means literally no limits in practice; most "unlimited" hosting claims are subject to a fair-use policy.

### H3: Performance & Uptime

Both are shared hosts, meaning performance depends partly on other sites sharing your server resources — true of any budget shared plan from any brand. Independent hosting benchmark and uptime-monitoring sites periodically test both; results can vary by test period and data center. **Don't rely on either company's own marketing claims about speed/uptime — check recent, dated independent benchmark data before deciding**, since this kind of data goes stale quickly and neither host's number should be taken as a permanent guarantee.

### H3: WordPress-Specific Support

Bluehost's longer history with WordPress-specific hosting and its past official WordPress.org recommendation give it strong documentation and community familiarity for WordPress beginners specifically. Hostinger has its own WordPress-optimized plans and has invested heavily in this area too — the gap here has narrowed, but Bluehost's WordPress-specific track record is longer.

### H3: Customer Support

Both offer live chat as their primary support channel; phone and ticket support availability vary by plan and have varied over time for both companies based on user reports. Support quality is one of the more commonly disputed points in budget-hosting reviews generally — treat any single review's support experience (including this one, since we haven't run a live, dated support test for this article) as anecdotal rather than definitive, and check recent independent reviews for current sentiment.

**Does Bluehost still have an official WordPress.org recommendation?**

**Which is better for a total beginner: Hostinger or Bluehost?**
