## H2: Honest Cons

- **Renewal price shock**: the price you pay after your first promotional term ends is noticeably higher than what you signed up for — budget for this explicitly, don't assume the intro price is permanent
- **Cheapest tier has real resource limits**: entry plans typically cap the number of websites you can host and impose bandwidth/CPU/storage limits that can pinch a growing site — check current specific limits on hostinger.com, don't assume "unlimited" language means literally unlimited in practice
- **Non-standard control panel**: hPanel is beginner-friendly but not cPanel — if you later want to migrate to a host that only supports cPanel-based migration tools, expect some extra manual work

- **Support quality varies by channel and time of day**: live chat is generally the fastest route; experiences vary by report, so don't assume phone-level support parity with pricier hosts

## H2: FAQ

**Is Hostinger actually cheap, or is that just the first-year price?**
The advertised low price is typically a promotional rate for an initial prepaid term. The renewal price is meaningfully higher. It can still be a good deal for a first site — just budget for the renewal rate, not the ad price, when deciding if it fits your long-term budget. Verify current numbers on hostinger.com.

**Does Hostinger use cPanel?**
No — Hostinger uses its own custom control panel, hPanel, on most plans. It's generally considered beginner-friendly, but it's a different interface than cPanel-based hosts.

**Is Hostinger good enough for a business site, or only hobby sites?**
It can work for a small business brochure site or blog on the entry tiers. If you expect meaningful traffic growth, ecommerce transactions at scale, or need guaranteed high-availability infrastructure, plan to evaluate higher Hostinger tiers (cloud/VPS) or a different provider as you grow — don't assume the cheapest shared tier scales indefinitely.

**Can Hostinger guarantee 100% uptime or my site never going down?**
No. No shared host can honestly guarantee zero downtime. Reasonable uptime on small shared plans is a realistic expectation; a literal 100% guarantee is not something to expect from any budget host — check current uptime data on independent monitoring sites, not marketing claims alone.
