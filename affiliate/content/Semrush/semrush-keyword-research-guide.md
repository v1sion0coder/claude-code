## H2: Before You Start — What You Actually Need

- A clear idea of your topic/niche (a "seed" concept — e.g., "home coffee brewing," not "coffee")
- Knowledge of your own site's current authority/traffic level (realistic targets differ for a brand-new site vs. an established one)
- About 30–60 minutes of focused time for the first pass; refining takes longer

You do **not** strictly need a paid tool to do basic keyword research — Google's autocomplete, "People Also Ask," and the free Google Keyword Planner (via a Google Ads account) can get you a starting list. What a tool like Semrush adds is speed and scale: pulling hundreds of related terms with volume/difficulty data in minutes instead of hours of manual searching.

## H2: Step 1 — Start With Seed Keywords

Write down 3–10 broad terms that describe what your content/business is about, from your own knowledge of the topic and your audience's likely language (not jargon only insiders use).

*Example seed terms for a fictional coffee blog:* "pour over coffee," "espresso machine," "coffee grinder."

---

## H2: Step 2 — Expand the List (Keyword Magic Tool)

Enter a seed term into Semrush's **Keyword Magic Tool**. It returns a large list of related keywords, typically grouped by sub-topic, along with columns for:
- **Search volume** (estimated monthly searches)
- **Keyword difficulty** (Semrush's modeled estimate of how hard it is to rank)
- **Search intent** (informational, commercial, transactional, navigational)
- **SERP features** (featured snippet, People Also Ask, etc. currently showing for that term)

**Honest caveat:** these numbers are modeled estimates from third-party data sources, not exact counts from Google. Treat them as directional — useful for comparing terms against each other, not as precise predictions. Cross-check your most important target terms against Google Search Console (if you have historical data) or Google Ads' own volume ranges.

Use the filters to narrow by:
- Intent (e.g., filter to "commercial" if you're targeting buyers, not just researchers)
- Volume range (exclude near-zero-volume terms unless you have a specific reason to target them)
- Word count (longer, more specific "long-tail" phrases are often lower-competition and easier wins for newer sites)

## H2: Step 3 — Group Keywords Into Topics, Not Just a List

Don't plan one article per keyword — that leads to thin, repetitive content that can cannibalize itself in search results. Instead, cluster related keywords (Semrush's Keyword Magic Tool groups many of these automatically) into topic clusters that map to a single, comprehensive page or article.

*Example:* "best pour over coffee maker," "pour over coffee maker recommendations," and "top pour over kettles" likely belong in one buyer's-guide-style article, not three separate thin posts.

---

## H2: Step 4 — Check Difficulty Against Your Site's Actual Authority

A keyword difficulty score is only useful relative to what your site can realistically compete for. A brand-new site with little authority will struggle even for "medium difficulty" terms that an established site could rank for easily.

Practical approach:
- New/low-authority sites: prioritize lower-difficulty, longer-tail terms first to build early wins and authority
- Established sites: can reasonably target higher-difficulty, higher-volume terms

Semrush's Keyword Overview report shows difficulty alongside the current top-ranking pages for that term — look at those pages yourself. If every top-10 result is a major, well-known domain with deep content, that's a signal the term will be hard to crack regardless of what the difficulty score says.

---

## H2: Step 5 — Analyze Search Intent by Looking at the Actual SERP

Don't rely on the intent label alone — open the search results for your top candidate keywords and look at what's actually ranking:
- Are top results blog posts, product pages, videos, or forum threads?
- Is there a featured snippet you could realistically target with a clear, structured answer?
- Are "People Also Ask" questions relevant subtopics you should address in the same piece?

This step matters because a keyword with great volume is wasted effort if the intent behind it doesn't match what you're offering (e.g., targeting a "free" or "DIY" keyword with a page trying to sell a paid product).

---

## H2: Step 6 — Check What Competitors Are Already Ranking For (Content Gap)

Semrush's **Keyword Gap** (content gap) tool lets you compare your domain against 1–4 competitor domains to see keywords they rank for that you don't. This surfaces realistic opportunities — terms proven to have ranking pages already, rather than pure guesses.

**Honest caveat:** a competitor ranking for a term doesn't mean you can or should chase it — factor in the difficulty and intent checks from Steps 4–5 before committing.

---

## H2: Step 7 — Prioritize and Build Your Content Calendar

With your clustered, intent-checked, difficulty-checked keyword groups in hand, prioritize by a combination of:
1. Realistic difficulty for your current site authority
2. Business relevance (does ranking for this term actually support a goal — leads, sales, brand awareness?)
3. Volume (all else equal, prefer higher volume)

Assign each cluster to one planned piece of content, and track it in a simple spreadsheet or your CMS's editorial calendar. Semrush's Position Tracking tool can then monitor how your published content actually performs against these target terms over time — useful for knowing what's working, not just what you hoped would work.

## H2: A Note on What Keyword Research Can't Do

Keyword research tells you *what people search for* and gives you a rough sense of competition. It does not:
- Guarantee you'll rank for any given term
- Guarantee traffic even if you do rank (click-through depends on your title/snippet, SERP features, and competing results)
- Replace the need for genuinely useful, well-written content

Be skeptical of anyone (including us) who implies otherwise.

## H2: FAQ

**Do I need a paid tool to do keyword research at all?**
No. Google's autocomplete, "People Also Ask," "related searches," and the free Google Keyword Planner (via a Google Ads account, no ad spend required to view it) can produce a workable starter list, especially for smaller sites or early-stage projects. Paid tools like Semrush mainly save time and add scale/difficulty scoring once you're managing many keywords or clients.

**How many keywords should one article target?**
Focus each article on one primary keyword/topic cluster, with a handful of closely related secondary terms addressed naturally in the content (e.g., via subheadings answering related questions). Don't force in unrelated terms just because they have volume.

**How often should I redo keyword research?**
Search behavior and competition shift over time — revisiting core topic areas every 6–12 months (or when you notice ranking/traffic changes) is a reasonable cadence for most sites.

**Is keyword difficulty from any tool 100% accurate?**
No — it's a modeled estimate based on backlink and ranking-page data, and different tools calculate it differently, which is why Semrush's, Ahrefs', and other tools' difficulty scores for the same term often don't match exactly. Use it as one directional input, not gospel.
