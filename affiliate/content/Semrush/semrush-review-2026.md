## H2: Core Features — What You Actually Get

### H3: Keyword Research (Keyword Magic Tool)
Lets you pull large keyword lists around a seed term, filtered by volume, difficulty, intent, and SERP features. Good for building topical clusters and content calendars at scale. As with any keyword tool, treat volume/difficulty numbers as directional estimates, not precise facts — cross-check important terms against Google Search Console or Google Ads data.

### H3: Position Tracking
Tracks your (or competitors') rankings for a defined keyword set over time, with location and device granularity. Useful for reporting to clients or leadership, though — as with all rank trackers — local pack and personalized results mean tracked positions won't always match what an individual searcher sees.

### H3: Site Audit
Crawls your site and flags technical issues: broken links, duplicate content, crawl errors, missing metadata, and more, with a prioritized "fix this first" list. Genuinely useful for catching issues a manual review would miss, especially on larger sites.

### H3: Backlink Analytics
Lets you audit your own or a competitor's backlink profile — referring domains, anchor text distribution, toxic link flags. Has improved substantially in past years, though independent link-building specialists often still supplement with Ahrefs for the deepest link research (see our [Semrush vs. Ahrefs comparison]).

### H3: On-Page & Content Tools
The SEO Writing Assistant and SEO Content Template give in-editor guidance (readability, keyword usage, tone, comparison against top-ranking competitor content) while you write. This is one of Semrush's genuine differentiators versus more link-focused competitors.

### H3: Advertising & Competitive Research
Lets you see (approximate, historical) data on competitors' paid search ads, ad copy, and landing pages. Useful for PPC teams doing competitive research, and not something every SEO suite offers.

## H2: Honest Cons

- **Not cheap, especially at scale**: costs increase meaningfully as you add projects, keywords tracked, or users — budget for this before committing to annual billing
- **Learning curve**: the sheer number of tools and reports can overwhelm new users in the first few sessions; plan to invest a few hours in tutorials

## H2: FAQ

**Is Semrush worth it for a solo freelancer?**
It can be, if you're actively billing SEO/content work to clients and the time saved justifies the subscription. If you're just starting out with limited paying clients, the cost may outweigh the benefit until your workload grows — consider starting with the free trial or a lower entry tier if available.

**Does Semrush have a free plan?**
Semrush has historically offered a limited free account tier plus paid trials of full plans; exact current free-tier limits and trial availability change over time — check semrush.com for what's currently offered.

**Is Semrush better than Ahrefs?**
Neither is universally "better" — they overlap heavily but differ in emphasis (Semrush: broader marketing suite + content tools; Ahrefs: deeper backlink research). See our full [Semrush vs. Ahrefs comparison] for a feature-by-feature breakdown.

**Can Semrush guarantee my site will rank higher?**
No. No SEO tool can guarantee rankings — rankings depend on many factors outside any tool's control, including competition, content quality, and search engine algorithm changes. Semrush provides data and workflow support, not outcomes.
